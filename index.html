The Opportunity

Most raw parcels never make it through. This one did.

Grove is ~30.7 acres across two contiguous parcels in one of the world's great cool-climate wine regions. The 25.9-acre main parcel, acquired in 2022, has since cleared a full Washington County entitlement process — drainage-hazard analysis, geotechnical slope-stability review, FEMA flood determination — culminating in issued grading and culvert permits in January 2026. A contiguous 4.76-acre parcel directly to the north rounds out the estate.

That matters because entitlement, not dirt, is where rural vineyard projects die. The regulatory path is slow, expensive, and uncertain. Grove is through it. What remains is execution on ground that is already cleared to build.

And Grove isn't raw land hunting for a concept — it comes with a going brand. Halem Heights, an established Oregon wine label, is part of the project today: the license, active distribution, and existing inventory are in hand. The land and a real brand, together.

Carrying cost is minimal. Both parcels sit under Oregon farm-use (EFU) tax deferral, so the only ongoing liability is the annual property tax — the main parcel's 2025 bill was just $526.

The vision is phased and patient: establish the vineyard, build the estate, and bring the wine home — each stage de-risking and adding value to the one before.

Entitled & de-risked

Permits in hand. Drainage hazard cleared with conditions (Case L2500008-DHA). Outside the FEMA flood hazard area. The approvals that stop most projects are done and on file.

Prime terroir

Elevation, slope, and natural drainage in the hills above Forest Grove — the same cool-climate conditions that made the Willamette Valley world-famous for Pinot Noir.

Phased upside

A staged plan — vineyard, then estate, then estate-grown wine under Halem Heights — that lets capital enter at a de-risked basis and participate in value created at each step.

The Story

Three years to a build-ready site

Building rural vineyard infrastructure in Washington County is a regulatory marathon. Here is the real path — from acquisition to permits in hand.

Jul 2022

Acquisition

Grove Vineyard, LLC purchases the main parcel on NW Timmerman Rd, financed with farm lending. The long-term vision begins.

2023

Entitlement begins — and gets hard

First approach to the county on the existing access and creek culvert. The county flags prior work; the full entitlement process opens.

Dec 2024

The gauntlet: Type II Drainage Hazard Analysis

Because a mapped stream crosses the parcel, the county requires a full drainage-hazard review — engineered, submitted, revised, and resubmitted.

Mar 2025 · Win

Drainage hazard approved · FEMA cleared

Case L2500008-DHA approved with conditions. FEMA confirms the parcel sits outside all flood hazard zones. The hardest approval is secured.

Jan 2026 · Win

Permits issued

Grading permit GRAD-2505132 and culvert permit BLDG-2514996 both issued and paid. The site is cleared to build.

2026 →

Site development

Access and site preparation underway — the foundation for vineyard establishment and everything that follows.
